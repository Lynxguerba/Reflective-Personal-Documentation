A self-conducted behavioral experiment on human social behavior and self-regulation. [Guerba, 2026]

Note: This study explores the intersection of physiological impairment and conscious self-regulation. Findings suggest that cognitive awareness can persist despite physical decline [1].

01

Abstract & Introduction

This study, conducted by Dinno Guerba (BSIT), formally recounts a personal experiential activity designed to analyze social behavior under the influence of substances. Unlike traditional observations that assume immediate loss of self-regulation [2], this experiment focuses on the threshold of conscious control.

02

1. Baseline Cognitive State

During the initial phase (21:00 - 22:00), cognitive functioning remained at a normal baseline. As noted in similar behavioral studies [3], the absence of impairment allows for a controlled transition into the active phase of the experiment.

03

2. Physiological Onset

At 23:00, mild dizziness was observed. However, the subject maintained complete awareness, contradicting the common social theory that dizziness is synonymous with a loss of emotional control [Guerba, 2026, Sec. 2].

04

3. Fatigue & Cognitive Persistence

Between 01:00 and 02:00, physical fatigue accumulated significantly. Despite this, the subject was still able to follow complex social conversations and make conscious decisions, supporting the hypothesis of independent cognitive self-regulation [4].

05

4. Peak Physical Impairment

At 02:00 - 04:00, physical decline reached its peak. The body signaled overexertion through intensive dizziness. Yet, the mental presence remained "intact and respectful" [Guerba, 2026], indicating that motor impairment does not strictly dictate social behavior.

07

Reflective Conclusion

The experiment concludes that while physiological responses are inevitable, the loss of self-regulation is often a social choice rather than a biological certainty [5]. This reinforces the importance of individual values in academic and professional social contexts.
